# Judge Q&A

**Q1. What exactly is a Coverage Cliff?**
A Coverage Cliff is a phenomenon where a model maintains high accuracy within the bounds of what it has seen during demonstrations, but abruptly drops to zero (or near-zero) accuracy when the task complexity exceeds that demonstrated coverage by a specific threshold.

**Q2. What is demonstration coverage?**
Demonstration coverage (Cmax) is the maximum structural complexity represented in the demonstrations given to the model.

**Q3. How is extrapolation distance calculated?**
Extrapolation Distance (Dext) is the difference between the test task's complexity (Ctest) and the demonstration coverage (Cmax). `Dext = Ctest - Cmax`.

**Q4. Why is ground truth trustworthy?**
Ground truth is generated independently using a Z3 mathematical solver, completely decoupled from the model/evaluator prediction pipeline. It is mathematically verified.

**Q5. Can the evaluator see the hidden rule?**
No. The evaluator strictly receives the same input representations (demonstrations and queries) that a standard AI model would receive.

**Q6. Is this a real AI model?**
The current Phase uses a deterministic reference evaluator, which acts as a predictable stand-in to validate the experimental infrastructure and boundary detection algorithms. It is not a frontier LLM.

**Q7. Does this prove AI has a universal Coverage Cliff?**
No. This demonstrates that our experiment can rigorously detect boundaries under specific conditions. It does not establish a universal law for all AI models or all tasks.

**Q8. Why use a deterministic reference evaluator?**
To build a scientifically robust, reproducible experimental engine. Real models have high variance, noise, and hidden training data. A reference evaluator guarantees that the testing infrastructure itself is mathematically sound before testing volatile commercial APIs.

**Q9. What happens if there is no cliff?**
The system's Cliff Detector classifies the accuracy curve appropriately—for example, as STABLE, GRADUAL DEGRADATION, or NON-MONOTONIC. The UI does not force a cliff narrative if none exists.

**Q10. What is the difference between a sharp cliff and gradual degradation?**
A sharp cliff is an abrupt accuracy drop (e.g., from 100% to 0% at a single extrapolation step). Gradual degradation is a smooth, continuous decline in accuracy as distance increases.

**Q11. Why does BDH-CQ belong in the project?**
BDH-CQ is a relevant frontier architecture that also studies demonstration-driven reasoning, specifically through latent state updates rather than written chain-of-thought traces. It provides high-level architectural context for our coverage experiment.

**Q12. Did you run official BDH-CQ?**
No. The BDH-CQ section relies on published evidence and an illustrative toy visualization, explicitly labeled as educational abstractions.

**Q13. Is the BDH-CQ equation official?**
The equation `h_(t+1) = f(h_t, x)` is a pedagogical simplification of recurrent latent updating, not the full continuous-time or biologically plausible equation used in the official BDH-CQ implementation.

**Q14. Is the BDH-CQ toy an official implementation?**
No, it is strictly an illustrative UI element designed to teach the concept of iterative latent state transformations.

**Q15. What would you do next?**
Integrate commercial LLMs via API, expand the task families (e.g., spatial reasoning), and test an official open-source BDH-CQ checkpoint on our Coverage Cliff benchmark.

**Q16. What is technically reusable about this system?**
The entire backend Experiment Engine, the strict separation between Evaluators and Ground Truth Solvers, and the statistical Cliff Detector module.

**Q17. What prevents data leakage?**
The architecture passes only sanitized `input_representation` strings to the Evaluator interface. The `hidden_rule` and `seed` variables remain isolated within the Generator and Solver layers.

**Q18. How reproducible is the experiment?**
100% reproducible. By entering the same Random Seed and Demonstration Coverage into the UI, the FastAPI backend will recompute the exact identical tasks and evaluations deterministically.

**Q19. What are the biggest limitations?**
Our reference evaluator is synthetic. A real LLM might exhibit different failure modes (like gradual degradation) rather than the sharp cliff generated by our deterministic test algorithm.

**Q20. What would falsify your claim?**
The claim would be challenged if controlled experiments across the specified task family and conditions repeatedly showed stable 100% accuracy across the tested extrapolation range, or if the apparent cliff disappeared under valid controls (e.g., different prompt engineering).
